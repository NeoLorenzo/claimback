Also worth doing

Suggestions from the Planner. Unlike the letter, these aren't fact-checked, so confirm products, prices and coverage before relying on them.

  • Have the endocrinologist's office call the plan for a peer-to-peer review or reconsideration. They should point out that the reviewer applied the pre-01/01/2026 version of MP-DME-031; this may reverse the denial faster than a formal appeal.
  • Ask whether the plan covers CGMs under the pharmacy benefit instead of DME. Many plans do, sometimes with different criteria. If so, submit through the pharmacy channel at the same time.
  • If the internal appeal is upheld, request independent external review. A denial based on superseded criteria is well suited to outside review.
  • If the plan is a fully insured or state-regulated product, file a complaint with the state insurance regulator about the plan applying outdated medical policy.
  • If the patient is actually enrolled in Medicare Advantage at age 65, cite CMS rules: MA plans must cover at least what Traditional Medicare covers. Medicare's CGM LCD covers insulin-treated patients or those with problematic hypoglycemia.
  • Look into manufacturer patient assistance or cash-price programs for the prescribed CGM as a bridge while the appeal is pending. Note that the OTC CGMs currently sold are generally not labeled for people on insulin or at risk of hypoglycemia, so they are not an appropriate substitute here; confirm this with the prescriber.
  • Meanwhile, the clinician may want to address nocturnal safety, for example a bedtime glucose check plan, a glucagon prescription, or insulin regimen review. This is clinical care, not a coverage route, and is the prescriber's decision.

Evidence relied on

  • EV-1 7. Diabetes Technology: Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026 (Diabetes Care 49(Suppl 1):S150) (American Diabetes Association): Recommendation 7.15 recommends CGM from diabetes onset onward for children, adolescents, and adults on insulin therapy (Grade A), on noninsulin therapies that can cause hypoglycemia (Grade C), and on any treatment where CGM helps management (Grade C). Recommendation 7.5 says people using CGM should have continued access to devices across third-party payors regardless of age or A1C (Grade E). In 2024, Rec. 7.15 said CGM 'should be offered' to adults on basal insulin. In 2026 the wording changed to 'recommended.' Section 9, Rec. 9.25, was updated to match.
  • EV-2 Summary of Revisions: Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026 (American Diabetes Association): The ADA summary of 2026 changes states that CGM benefits have been shown regardless of age, sex, education or income level, or baseline diabetes characteristics.
  • EV-3 6. Glycemic Goals, Hypoglycemia, and Hyperglycemic Crises: Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026 (American Diabetes Association): The 2026 text says CGM is particularly useful for people with diabetes at risk for hypoglycemia. For older adults with complex or intermediate health, the CGM targets allow more time high (time above 250 mg/dL under 10%) because hypoglycemia is the greater danger. Table 6.4 defines level 2 hypoglycemia as glucose below 54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L).
  • EV-4 6. Glycemic Goals and Hypoglycemia: Standards of Care in Diabetes—2024 (American Diabetes Association (via PubMed Central)): The 2024 edition's Rec. 6.11d stated that CGM is beneficial and recommended for people at high risk for hypoglycemia (Grade A). Rec. 6.15 stated that one or more level 2 or 3 hypoglycemia episodes should prompt reevaluation of the treatment plan (Grade E).
  • EV-5 Local Coverage Determination L33822: Glucose Monitors (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services): [UNVERIFIED — the researcher reported this from memory] CGM is covered when the patient has diabetes, has been trained to use the device, the CGM is prescribed according to its FDA indications, and a qualifying visit occurred within the prior 6 months. The patient must also meet either (A) insulin-treated, including basal-only, or (B) problematic hypoglycemia. Problematic hypoglycemia means recurrent level 2 events despite more than one attempt to adjust treatment, or one level 3 event.
