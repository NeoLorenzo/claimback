The denial states:

“Per Evergreen Mutual Medical Policy MP-DME-031 (Continuous Glucose Monitoring), coverage requires that the member be treated with intensive insulin therapy consisting of three (3) or more daily injections of insulin or use of an insulin pump. The submitted records indicate the member uses a once-daily long-acting insulin only.”

MP-DME-031 was last revised on 01/01/2026. Its revision history states:

“01/01/2026 - Criteria I.B revised to align with updated national coverage criteria and professional society guidelines. Requirement for three or more daily insulin injections REMOVED. Coverage now extends to members using any insulin, or members with a documented history of problematic hypoglycemia.”

My CGM request was submitted on September 18, 2026, more than eight months after this revision. The three-injection and insulin-pump requirement does not appear anywhere in the current policy text. The denial found that I use a once-daily long-acting insulin. That is exactly the regimen current Criterion I.B.1 expressly covers:

“The member is insulin-treated (any insulin regimen, including a single daily dose of basal/long-acting insulin).”

The denial therefore rests on a standard that was not in effect on the date of my request. Applied correctly, the Plan's own finding confirms that Criterion I.B is met.

4. Supporting Guidelines and Evidence

The Plan's own 01/01/2026 revision states that Criterion I.B was revised to align with updated national coverage criteria and professional society guidelines. The sources below are offered for that reason: they show the current criteria reflect the prevailing standard of care. They are not offered as a substitute for the Plan's policy.

American Diabetes Association (ADA) Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026

  • The 2026 Standards recommend CGM at diagnosis and at any time afterward for adults using insulin. [EV-1]
  • The Older Adults section recommends CGM for older adults with type 2 diabetes on insulin to improve glucose control, reduce hypoglycemia, and reduce treatment burden. It also calls for screening for and addressing hypoglycemia at every visit, because older adults on insulin are at higher risk. [EV-3]
  • The Diabetes Technology section reports that randomized trials in type 2 diabetes on insulin, including basal insulin, consistently show lower A1c and more time in range (70–180 mg/dL) with CGM. It describes the evidence for fingerstick monitoring in people on basal insulin alone as insufficient. [EV-2]

Clinical significance of my low readings

The ADA and EASD have adopted a three-level hypoglycemia classification [EV-11]. Under that classification, glucose below 54 mg/dL was chosen as the clinically important threshold, in part because cognitive impairment begins below it [EV-10]. My readings of 48 and 51 mg/dL fall below this threshold.

Randomized trial evidence in basal-insulin type 2 diabetes

  • The MOBILE randomized trial (JAMA 2021) enrolled 175 adults with poorly controlled type 2 diabetes on basal insulin without mealtime insulin. It compared 8 months of real-time CGM with fingerstick monitoring. Its population closely matches my situation. [EV-12]
  • In the MOBILE extension, time in range rose from 38% to 62% on CGM. About half of that gain was lost within 6 months of stopping CGM. [EV-13]
  • A 3-month analysis of MOBILE found that time in range improved within about 7 days of starting CGM, with no increase in hypoglycemia at 3 months. This supports my endocrinologist's plan to use CGM data to adjust insulin safely. [EV-14]

National coverage standard

Medicare's Glucose Monitors LCD (L33822) covers therapeutic CGM for patients who are insulin-treated or who have problematic hypoglycemia. It also requires an in-person or Medicare-approved telehealth visit [EV-4]. CMS compliance guidance confirms that being insulin-treated is sufficient to qualify [EV-7]. The evidence review behind Medicare's 2023 coverage expansion specifically examined whether CGM benefits people who do not take insulin three or more times daily [EV-5]. The Plan's 01/01/2026 removal of the three-injection requirement is consistent with that national standard.

  • EV-1 Summary of Revisions: Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026 (American Diabetes Association, Diabetes Care 49(Suppl 1):S6): Recommendation 7.15 (repeated as 9.25) now recommends CGM at diagnosis and at any time afterward for adults using insulin, adults on non-insulin medications that can cause hypoglycemia, or adults on any regimen where CGM is beneficial.   • EV-2 7. A systematic review and meta-analysis supports benefits for glucose control, patient experience, health care resource use and cost-effectiveness. About half of the gains are lost when people on basal insulin stop CGM. The ADA calls the evidence for fingerstick monitoring on basal insulin alone insufficient. The trials did not consistently show fewer level 2 hypoglycemia events.
  • EV-3 13. Older Adults: Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026 (American Diabetes Association (via PMC)): Recommendation 13.4 calls for screening for and addressing hypoglycemia at every visit because older adults on insulin are at higher risk. Recommendation 13.5 (new for 2026, grade B) recommends CGM for older adults with type 2 diabetes on insulin to improve glucose control, reduce hypoglycemia and reduce treatment burden. The chapter notes CGM is effective and acceptable in this group, and that long-acting insulins such as glargine carry lower hypoglycemia ED-visit and hospitalization risk than NPH in Medicare patients.
  • EV-4 LCD L33822: Glucose Monitors (CMS / DME MACs): Initial CGM coverage requires all of the following: a diabetes diagnosis; the prescriber's conclusion that the patient or caregiver is adequately trained; prescribing consistent with FDA labeling; insulin treatment OR problematic hypoglycemia; and an in-person or Medicare-approved telehealth visit. Problematic hypoglycemia means recurrent (more than one) level 2 events (<54 mg/dL) that persist despite more than one attempt to adjust treatment, or at least one level 3 event.
  • EV-5 Proposed LCD: Glucose Monitors (DL39473) (CMS): This proposed revision underlies the 2023 coverage expansion. Its evidence review asked whether CGM benefits beneficiaries who do not take insulin three or more times daily. The final policy uses "insulin-treated" with no requirement on injection count or dosing frequency.
  • EV-6 Glucose Monitor Policy Article A52464 (CMS / DME MACs): Medicare has covered therapeutic (non-adjunctive) CGMs as durable medical equipment since January 12, 2017. Sensors and related supplies are covered through a monthly supply allowance. The article lists the qualifying ICD-10 codes.
  • EV-7 Glucose Monitoring Supplies (Medicare Provider Compliance Tips) (CMS Medicare Learning Network): CMS compliance guidance confirms that being insulin-treated is sufficient to qualify. After starting CGM, the prescriber must see the patient in person or by approved telehealth every 6 months and document adherence.
  • EV-8 esMD Home Blood Glucose Monitoring Reason Codes and Statements (June 2025) (CMS): Lists the standard denial reasons, including no documented insulin treatment and hypoglycemia events not documented with glucose values and level.

Lows without symptoms suggest impaired hypoglycemia awareness. The paper frames severe hypoglycemia as a preventable cardiovascular risk factor.
  • EV-10 Cognitive effects of hypoglycemia (Diabetes Care 2022;45(9):2103) (Diabetes Care (American Diabetes Association)): Explains that the IHSG chose <54 mg/dL as the clinically important threshold partly because cognitive impairment begins below it.
