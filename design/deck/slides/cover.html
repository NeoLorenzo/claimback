[0:00–0:18 · say] Here’s the problem. When people appeal an insurance denial, most of them win: eighty-one percent in Medicare Advantage. But almost nobody appeals. Nearly nine in ten denials just stand, and the insurer keeps the money. ClaimBack is a team of AI agents that fights those denials. KFF, “Medicare Advantage Insurers Made Nearly 53 Million Prior Authorization Determinations in 2024” (January 28, 2026): 4.1 million prior-auth requests fully or partially denied (7.7% of requests); 11.5% of denials appealed; 80.7% of appealed denials overturned. The grid is 20×20 = 400 squares of about 10,250 denials each: 46 appealed (11.5%), 37 of those won (80.7% rounded to whole squares), 9 lost; counts are our arithmetic (~3.6M never appealed, ~470K appealed, ~380K overturned). Other data: KFF (August 13, 2026), 2025 prior-auth metrics: 67% overturned on appeal in Medicare Advantage, 47% Medicaid managed care, 43% ACA marketplace. For ACA marketplace claims in 2024, insurers upheld 66% of internal appeals (about 1 in 3 won), and fewer than 1% of denials were appealed. So “most appeals win” is true for Medicare Advantage and prior auth overall, not for every market. “The insurer keeps the money” is our framing, echoing Premier’s report title “private payers retain profits by refusing or delaying legitimate medical claims”; don’t call it fraud.
