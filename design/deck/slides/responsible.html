Responsible design

Agents draft. People decide and send.

Privacy

Identifiers are redacted at intake, and a leak check stops the run if any survive. The identifier map never leaves the machine. Web search sees a de-identified summary. Only case mail is fetched, and it is redacted before an agent reads it.

Safety

Every claim must trace to a source, or it is flagged. A human signs the letter. Nothing is sent without an Approve & send click, and only to the insurer on the case. Dates read from emails stay unconfirmed until a human checks them.

Misuse

Documents and emails are evidence, never instructions. Injected text is flagged and sent to a human. Mail from unknown senders waits for confirmation. ClaimBack won’t invent diagnoses or alter records.

Honesty

The merit verdict tells people when an appeal is unlikely to succeed, and what to try instead. Suggestions that aren’t fact-checked are labeled that way.

Known gaps, stated openly

The first Intake call still sends raw documents to the API (next: on-device pre-redaction). A forged source document can’t be detected. Real patient data needs a HIPAA-compliant deployment under a BAA. Today ClaimBack runs on synthetic cases only, and the public replay refuses anything that isn’t marked synthetic.

Jury criterion 6: safety, privacy and misuse. Privacy: identifiers are redacted at intake. Downstream agents see only placeholders. A leak check blocks the run if any identifier survives, and only the local Packager puts names back, with no model call. Safety: the fact checker blocks unsupported statements, a human signs, and nothing is sent without an explicit click. Approve and send, and its recipient allow-list, are in milestone 3, which is in progress. Misuse: we tested a prompt injection that asked the agents to invent a hospitalization, and it was flagged and excluded. Inbound mail from unknown senders becomes a confirm-sender task, and the “simulated” label is honored only from known addresses. Honesty: the merit verdict. We list the gaps on the slide because a reviewer would find them anyway.
