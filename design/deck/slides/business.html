Jury criterion 3: business viability. The pricing is a hypothesis we want to test. Premier measured $43.84 in administrative cost to work one denial, without clinical labor, so $25 per appeal saves a clinic money on every case, and our measured model cost of $1.40 to $3.10 per run leaves a wide margin. Premier also estimates about 450 million denied claims a year across private payers, so even a small share is a real business. The competitors are real and verified. Claimable charges patients $39.95 per appeal for about 85 drugs, and mails and faxes the appeal for them. Counterforce Health is free and grant-funded, with a voice assistant in beta. Fight Health Insurance is free or pay what you want, with on-device OCR. Waystar AltitudeAI sells enterprise revenue-cycle software that generates appeal packages in bulk. Their success rates are self-reported, so we don’t compare against them. What we do differently: an adversarial reviewer and a fact checker before anything ships, a verdict that says when not to appeal, redaction before agents see the case, and a case that keeps running in a real inbox after the letter goes out.
