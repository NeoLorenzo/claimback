# Offline reference library (fallback for the Evidence Researcher)

Short paraphrased summaries kept on disk so a run can finish if live web search
fails. Each one was checked against its source in October 2026. Check the source
again before relying on it in a real appeal.

## REF-ADA-SOC-2026-7: ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026, Section 7 (Diabetes Technology)
Citation: Diabetes Care 2026;49(Suppl. 1):S150-S165, doi:10.2337/dc26-S007
Summary source: Summary of Revisions, Diabetes Care 2026;49(Suppl. 1):S6-S12 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690167)
Summary: Recommendation 7.15 recommends CGM at diabetes onset and anytime after
for children, adolescents and adults who are on insulin therapy, on non-insulin
therapies that can cause hypoglycemia, or on any diabetes treatment where CGM
helps in management. Table 7.3 lists real-time CGM, over-the-counter CGM and
professional CGM as the device types.

## REF-ADA-SOC-2026-9: ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026, Section 9 (Pharmacologic Approaches to Glycemic Treatment)
Citation: Diabetes Care 2026;49(Suppl. 1), doi:10.2337/dc26-S009
Summary: Recommendation 9.25 recommends CGM at diabetes onset and anytime after for
adults on insulin therapy, on non-insulin therapies that can cause hypoglycemia, or
on any treatment where CGM aids management. Figure 9.5 was revised to suggest
considering CGM for people with type 2 diabetes on basal insulin.

## REF-ADA-SOC-2026-13: ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026, Section 13 (Older Adults)
Citation: Diabetes Care 2026;49(Suppl. 1), doi:10.2337/dc26-S013
Summary: Recommendation 13.5 recommends CGM for older adults with type 1 diabetes, or
type 2 diabetes on insulin, to improve glycemic outcomes, reduce hypoglycemia and
reduce treatment burden.

## REF-CMS-LCD-L33822: CMS Local Coverage Determination L33822, Glucose Monitors
URL: https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/lcd.aspx?lcdid=33822
Summary: Medicare covers a CGM when the beneficiary has diabetes, has been trained
on the device, the CGM is prescribed per its FDA indications to improve glycemic
control, and the beneficiary is insulin-treated (any insulin; the earlier
multiple-daily-injection requirement was removed in the revision effective
04/16/2023) or has problematic hypoglycemia: recurrent (more than one) level 2
events (glucose < 54 mg/dL) that persist despite multiple (more than one) attempts
to adjust medication or the treatment plan, or one level 3 event needing
third-party help. The treating practitioner must have seen the beneficiary in
person or by telehealth within 6 months before ordering, and every 6 months after
to document adherence. When a CGM (E2102 or E2103) is covered, its supply
allowance (A4238 or A4239) is covered too.

## REF-ADA-HYPO: ADA Standards of Care, Section 6 (Glycemic Goals, Hypoglycemia and Hyperglycemic Crises)
Citation: Diabetes Care 2026;49(Suppl. 1), doi:10.2337/dc26-S006
Summary: Level 2 hypoglycemia is glucose below 54 mg/dL. Level 3 is a severe event
with altered mental or physical status requiring assistance. Recurrent or
unrecognized hypoglycemia should prompt a review of the treatment plan.
