I am the treating endocrinologist for Rosa Delgado, whom I have followed for type 2
diabetes mellitus. I am writing to document the medical necessity of a therapeutic
continuous glucose monitor (CGM).

Ms. Delgado is insulin-treated. She has used insulin glargine since 2024 and currently
takes 32 units subcutaneously each night. Her hemoglobin A1c was 9.1% on 09/10/2026,
up from 8.7% in March 2026.

She has had two documented symptomatic nocturnal hypoglycemic episodes below 54 mg/dL:
48 mg/dL on 07/02/2026 and 51 mg/dL on 08/19/2026. I reduced her glargine dose from 36
to 32 units after the first episode; the second episode occurred despite that
reduction. She lives alone, and peripheral neuropathy has reduced the sensation in her
fingertips, which makes frequent fingerstick testing difficult and unreliable for
detecting overnight lows.

At her 09/10/2026 visit I evaluated her diabetes control and determined that she meets
Evergreen Mutual Medical Policy MP-DME-031 criteria I.A through I.D: she has diabetes
mellitus, she is insulin-treated, the CGM is prescribed in accordance with its FDA
indications, and she was trained on the device by our diabetes educator that day.

A CGM will let us detect nocturnal hypoglycemia and safely titrate her basal insulin to
improve glycemic control. In my clinical judgment it is medically necessary.
